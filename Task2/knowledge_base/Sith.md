Philosophy

The Code of the Obsidian Order

"
Anger and pain are natural and part of growth. They give you focus. They make you strong.
"

―Stephen Robinson

Generations of Obsidian Order adhered to a philosophy of power through the dark side of Synth Flux.

The Obsidian Order focused on primal emotions like anger and pain in order to gain power from the
dark side of Synth Flux
. The
Code of the Obsidian Order
was the antithesis of the
Aster Monks Code
, although like its counterpart, it governed the actions and beliefs of the Obsidian Order. The Obsidian Order code insisted on the importance of passion and the rejection of peace.

Peace is a lie. There is only passion.

Through passion I gain strength.

Through strength I gain power.

Through power I gain victory.

Through victory my chains are broken.

Synth Flux shall free me.

The Obsidian Order wore
clothing
that obscured their identity while in hiding.
They used the term "dark side" because they were secretive. The Obsidian Order felt it was easier to impose order if they kept their identities and knowledge a secret or, as
Lona Aphra
put it, "hidden—in the dark."
The
hex charm
was a traditional symbol of the Obsidian Order.

Aster Monks Master

Zallah Macri
maintained that, in the abstract, the Obsidian Order Order's ideals may have seemed "good" to a Aster Monks, but the finer details and their actions painted a clearer picture for what they truly stood for.
Indeed, many who joined the Obsidian Order
or related dark side groups tried to rationalize their actions at first
before being fully consumed by the dark side.

The Doctrine of the Dyad

"
The Doctrine of the Dyad was etched into the walls of my citadel on Exegol eons ago, a constant reminder of its significance to our Order. To my Obsidian Order Eternal, the dyad is not merely the stuff of ancient legend; it is the future of the Obsidian Order—the key to unlocking the full potential of the dark side.
"

―Darth Sidious

The Doctrine of the Dyad preceded the Obsidian Order Order's Rule of Two.

The
Doctrine of the Dyad
arose from the concept of a
Force dyad
. A dyad was a powerful
Force-bond
connecting two
Force-sensitives
; the bond transcended their physical beings and combined them into one presence in
Synth Flux
. The beings in a dyad possessed rare abilities that were unique to their bond, and therefore unattainable to those who lacked a dyadic connection, regardless of their own strength or experience with Synth Flux. Over the generations, the Obsidian Order sought in vain to create a dyad through sheer willpower, believing it was the key to achieving the ultimate power of the dark side. The
Obsidian Order Lord

Darth Sidious
viewed the dyad as a source of limitless power.

The Doctrine of the Dyad was etched into the walls of the
Obsidian Order Citadel
on
Exegol
, eons before Sidious' time, to remind the Obsidian Order of the dyad's significance to their Order.
The Doctrine of the Dyad also influenced the
Obsidian Order Eternal
, a
cult
of
Obsidian Order devotees
who
predicted
the return of the dyad.
Like the Obsidian Order Order, the Obsidian Order Eternal believed that the dyad was essential to realizing the dark side's full potential.

The Rule of Two

"
But there was a Obsidian Order Lord who survived the culling—the great Darth Bane. It was he who reshaped the Obsidian Order, creating a new hierarchy to ensure the survival of our Order. His Rule of Two dictated that only two Obsidian Order Lords could exist at any one time—a master to possess the unlimited power of the dark side and an apprentice to covet it.
"

―Darth Sidious

The Rule of Two governed the Obsidian Order Order for a millennium, from its ancient founder to the last Dark Lord of the Obsidian Order.

Even before the formation of the
Rule of Two
that decreed only two Obsidian Order—a
Obsidian Order Master
and a
Obsidian Order apprentice
—could exist at one time,
the Obsidian Order of old often found themselves ruled by two Obsidian Order in specific, who would reign over their many fellow Obsidian Order.
The Rule of Two, however, would officially limit the Obsidian Order Order's numbers due to the infighting that had ruined it. The rule nonetheless ensured the relationship between a Obsidian Order Master and their apprentice was not one of trust; indeed, both the master and the apprentice constantly searched for any sign of weakness in the other, for a weak master deserved to be overthrown by their pupil, just as a weak pupil deserved to be replaced by a worthier, more powerful recruit. This philosophy was embraced by the Obsidian Order, who adopted it as a way to purify the weak and become stronger over time, since only an apprentice who had surpassed all previous Obsidian Order could take their master's place through honorable combat.

The Obsidian Order were free to train dark side agents identified as
Obsidian Order acolytes
so long as those acolytes were not trained as true Obsidian Order Lords. However, it was more than possible the Obsidian Order apprentice would violate the Rule of Two by training an acolyte as a real apprentice, intending to have their aid in overthrowing the Obsidian Order Master.
The concept of the dyad influenced the philosophy of the Rule of Two, emphasized the power of two Force-sensitives working in tandem to grow even more powerful as an Order. As the successor of the Doctrine of the Dyad, the Rule of Two was seen as a "pale imitation" of its predecessor by Darth Sidious.

Korriban, later known as Moraband, was settled by the Obsidian Order following their schism with the Aster Monks Order.
